When women business owners come to me for a new website, one of the first things they say is some version of "I know I need more pages."

More pages for every service. A separate page for their story. A resources page, a testimonials page, a FAQ page, a blog they plan to start "soon."

I understand the instinct. A bigger site feels more established. But in my experience, especially for coaches and service providers, a well-built three-page website often does more for your business than a sprawling one.

### Your visitors aren't there to explore

Most people who land on your website want to answer three questions quickly:

- Can she help me with my problem?
- How does working with her actually work?
- How do I take the next step?

Most of them decide whether to stay or leave in under a minute, without reading every page.

Every extra page is another place for that decision to get lost. When your services are spread across five pages and your booking link sits in the footer, you're asking a busy visitor to do the work of piecing it all together. Many won't.

### The three pages that matter most

A simple, focused site usually looks like this:

**1. Home**
This is where you speak directly to the person you serve. Name her problem, show her you understand it, and give her a clear sense of the result she can expect. A short introduction to you belongs here too, so you don't necessarily need a separate About page at the start.

**2. Services**
One page that lays out what you offer, who each option is for, and what working together looks like. Clear headings make it easy to scan. If you have two or three offers, they can live comfortably side by side.

**3. Contact or Booking**
A simple page with one obvious next step, whether that's booking a discovery call, filling out a short form, or both. No hunting required.

### A site you can keep up with beats a site you can't

Big websites also tend to go stale, and it's rarely talked about.

That events page from 2023. The "coming soon" blog. A services page still listing a package you stopped offering a year ago. When visitors spot outdated information, they quietly wonder whether you're still active, and that small doubt can be enough to send them elsewhere.

With three pages, keeping things current is realistic. You can review your whole site in one sitting, update a price or an offer in minutes, and feel confident that everything a potential client sees is accurate.

### Fewer pages means clearer messaging

When you only have three pages to work with, you have to get specific. What do you actually help with? Who is it really for? What should someone do next?

That focus is a gift. It forces the kind of clarity that makes a website convert. On a lot of the sites I review, the message is spread so thin that nothing stands out.

### When you might need more

A three-page site isn't the right fit for everyone, and that's okay. You might want additional pages if:

- You have several distinct offers that each need detailed explanation
- You sell products or digital courses through your site
- You're actively publishing blog content for SEO
- You run workshops or events with their own registration details

The good news is that a well-built three-page site is easy to grow. You can start focused and add pages when your business genuinely calls for them, rather than building them all upfront and hoping they get used.

### Start with what works

If your current website feels overwhelming to manage, or you've been putting off a new one because it seems like a huge project, a simpler site may be exactly what you need. A site that's current and easy to act on will serve you far better than a big one that's out of date.
