When I look at the analytics for a coach or service provider's website, the About page is almost always one of the most visited pages. People land on the home page, like what they see, and then click through to find out who they'd actually be working with.

And yet it's often the page owners struggle with most. Writing about yourself feels awkward, so it either turns into a long life story or gets two polite sentences and a headshot.

The good news is that a strong About page follows a fairly simple pattern. These are the pieces I'd include.

### Start with the person you help

The first few lines of your About page matter most. Many visitors will read them and decide whether to keep going.

Opening with a sentence about who you help and what you help them with gives a visitor something to recognize straight away. For example:

- "I help women in their first year of business stop second-guessing their pricing."
- "I work with busy moms who want to get back into running without getting injured."

Your own story comes next, and it lands better once the reader already knows this page is for her.

### Share the part of your story that explains your work

Your story matters. Visitors want to know you've been where they are, or that you understand it well.

The trick is choosing the parts that connect to what you do now. If you left a corporate job to start a coaching business and now help other women do the same, that's the story to tell. Where you went to school or your first job out of college can usually stay out, unless they explain why you're good at this.

A few short paragraphs is plenty. If it starts to read like a memoir, that's a sign to trim.

### Add a real photo of you

People want to see who they'll be talking to on a call. A warm, current photo of your face does more for trust than anything else on the page.

If you don't have professional photos yet, a well-lit phone photo works. I wrote about how to get a good one in [No Professional Photos? What to Use on Your Website Instead](/post/no-professional-photos-what-to-use-on-your-website-instead).

### Explain how you work

Somewhere on the page, give visitors a sense of what working with you looks like. That might be:

- How you work (one-to-one, small groups, online or in person)
- Who you're the best fit for, and who you might not be
- What you believe about your area of work, in a sentence or two

That last point is often the most persuasive part of an About page. A clear point of view helps the right clients feel at home and gently lets the wrong ones move on.

### Include a little proof

You don't need a wall of credentials. One or two short client quotes, a relevant certification or the number of years you've been doing this work can reassure a visitor that she's in good hands.

Only use quotes from real clients, with their permission. If you're just starting out and don't have any yet, that's fine. A clear description of how you work and a friendly photo will carry the page until you do.

### Add a personal detail or two

A small human detail makes you easier to remember: the dog who sits in on your video calls, or the city you're writing from this month. Keep it to a line or two, near the end, so it adds warmth without pulling focus.

### End with a clear next step

Many About pages simply stop at the bottom, and a visitor who was ready to reach out has to go hunting for how.

A short line inviting her to book a call, with a button right there, keeps the momentum going. If you'd like more on making that step easy, [How to Turn Website Visitors Into Discovery Calls](/post/how-to-turn-website-visitors-into-discovery-calls) walks through it.

### A quick checklist

Before you publish, it's worth reading your About page as if you were a potential client and checking that it:

- Says who you help in the first few lines
- Tells the part of your story that explains your work
- Shows a real, current photo of you
- Gives a sense of how you work and what you believe
- Ends with an easy way to get in touch
