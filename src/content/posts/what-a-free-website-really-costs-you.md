You've probably seen the ad. A professional website for your business, built for free. No upfront cost, no catch, just fill in a form and give up your email.

I saw one in my own feed this week, and as someone who builds websites for a living, I was curious. How does a company give away the thing it sells?

Short answer: it doesn't. The build is free. The website is not. Here's how these offers work, what they cost over time, and what to ask before you sign anything.

### Where the money actually comes from

A free website company still pays designers, developers and servers. That money comes back to them in one of a few ways.

- **A monthly fee.** The design is free, but hosting or "maintenance" runs $150 to $250 a month. This is the most common setup by far.
- **A minimum contract.** Most of these plans lock you in for 12 months or more, with an exit fee if you leave early.
- **Ownership stays with them.** Some contracts only license the site to you while you keep paying. Cancel, and the website (sometimes even your domain name) stays behind.
- **Upsells.** SEO packages, logos, booking tools, extra pages. The free site gets you in the door.

None of this is a scam. Some business owners are genuinely happy paying a monthly fee for someone else to handle everything. The problem is when "free" is the headline and the rest lives in the fine print.

### The math nobody puts in the ad

Here's what a "free" website costs at the monthly rates US agencies currently advertise.

| Monthly fee | After 1 year | After 2 years | After 3 years |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $150 | $1,800 | $3,600 | $5,400 |
| $199 | $2,388 | $4,776 | $7,164 |
| $249 | $2,988 | $5,976 | $8,964 |

By year two, most of these plans have cost more than a custom website paid for upfront. And if the contract says the site belongs to them, three years of payments can leave you with nothing to take with you.

Spreading the cost of a website over time is perfectly sensible. Spreading it over forever, for something you never own, is where it gets expensive.

### 5 questions to ask before you sign

Whether it's a free website, a pay-monthly plan or a traditional build, these five questions will tell you most of what you need to know.

1. **Is the domain registered in my name, with my own login?** Your domain is your address online. If someone else controls it, so do they.
2. **Who owns the design, code and content when the contract ends?** Look for the word "license." A licensed site is one you're renting.
3. **What's the minimum term, and what does it cost to leave early?** "Cancel anytime" and "12-month minimum" can both appear in the same contract.
4. **What exactly does the monthly fee include?** Edits, new pages, email, security updates, support. Find out what counts as extra before you need it.
5. **What's the total I'll pay?** If nobody can give you a number with an end date, the answer is "as long as you stay."

A good provider will answer all five without hesitating. If the answers get vague, that tells you something too.

### Is there an alternative to a free website where you actually own it?

Yes. There are three common routes, and all three leave the site in your name:

- **Pay for a build up front.** A designer builds the site, then hands the domain, code and content over to your own accounts. After that, hosting can cost very little.
- **A payment plan with an end date.** The same handover, with the cost spread over a set number of months. Once the last payment is made, the site is yours.
- **Build it yourself on a website builder.** Wix and Squarespace keep the hosting, but the account, the domain and your words are yours. You can take your content and domain elsewhere if you leave.

The question that separates these from a "free" website is the same every time: when the payments stop, does the website stay with you?
