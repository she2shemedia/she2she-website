Last week I asked on LinkedIn what you'd most like help with, and the answer was clear: getting found on Google.

It makes sense. You can have a beautiful website, but if the only people visiting it are the ones you've personally sent the link to, it isn't doing much work for you. The good news is that showing up on Google isn't reserved for big companies with marketing teams. Most of what helps a small service business show up is simple, and much of it you can do in an afternoon.

Here's where I'd start.

### Claim your Google Business Profile

If you only do one thing from this post, make it this one.

Your Google Business Profile is the box that appears on the right side of search results (or at the top on a phone) with your name, hours, reviews and a link to your site. It's free, and for local searches it often shows up before any website does.

Even if you work online with clients everywhere, a profile helps Google confirm you're a real, active business. Fill in every section you can: your services, a short description, your website, photos of you and your work. Then check in once a month or so to add a new photo or a quick update. An active profile tends to show up more often than one that was set up and forgotten.

### Tell Google what each page is about

Every page on your website has a page title. It's the blue headline people click on in search results, and it's one of the strongest clues Google uses to understand a page.

On many small business sites, the home page title is just the business name, or worse, "Home." That tells Google almost nothing.

A better page title says what you do and who you do it for. For example:

- Instead of "Home," try "Career Coaching for Women in Tech | Jane Smith Coaching"
- Instead of "Services," try "Business Coaching Packages for New Founders"

Use the words your clients would actually type into Google, not the clever name you gave your signature program. They're searching for "confidence coach for women," not "The Radiance Method."

### Write the way your clients search

The same idea applies to the words on your pages. If you help women leave corporate jobs and start a business, those words should appear naturally on your home page and services page.

You don't need to stuff keywords into every sentence. In fact, please don't. Just make sure your website plainly says what you do, who it's for and where you're based (if location matters to your business). Clear writing helps Google and your visitors at the same time.

### Make your details match everywhere

Google cross-checks your business information across the web. If your website says one thing, your Google profile says another and an old directory listing has a phone number you stopped using in 2022, that inconsistency can quietly work against you.

Take a few minutes to check that your business name, email, phone number and location (if you list one) match on your website, Google Business Profile, social media profiles and any directories you've joined.

### Ask for reviews

Reviews help in two ways. They give Google more confidence in your business, and they give the person reading them a reason to choose you.

The easiest way to get them is simply to ask. When a client tells you how much your work helped, that's the perfect moment to send your review link with a short, friendly note. Most happy clients are glad to help; they just need the nudge.

### Make sure your site is easy to use on a phone

Most searches now happen on phones, and Google pays attention to how your site works there. If text is tiny, buttons are hard to tap or pages take forever to load, visitors leave, and Google notices.

Pull up your own website on your phone and try booking a call. If anything feels clunky, that's worth fixing. If you'd like a few more ideas on what keeps visitors from taking the next step, [Why Your Website Isn't Converting](/post/why-your-website-isn-t-converting) covers the most common culprits.

### Give it time

One thing to know before you start: Google doesn't respond overnight. Changes to your page titles or profile can take a few weeks to show up, and building real visibility usually takes a few months of steady attention.

So it's worth starting now. Three months from today, the people searching for exactly what you offer will have a much better chance of finding you.
