Most business owners reach a point where something about their online presence feels off. Maybe you hesitate before sending someone your website link. Maybe you've stopped mentioning it altogether. You know it isn't working the way it should, but it's hard to say exactly why.

The tricky part is working out what actually needs fixing. Is it the website? Or is it the brand underneath it? The two are easy to confuse, and the fix for one is quite different from the fix for the other.

A quick way to separate them: your **brand** is your colors, fonts, logo and overall look and feel, and it shows up everywhere, from Instagram to your email signature to the proposals you send. Your **website** is one place your brand lives, plus the structure, wording and booking path that turn a visitor into a client.

### Signs the brand is the problem

- You picked your colors and fonts quickly, years ago, or from whatever the DIY tool offered, and they don't reflect who you serve now.
- Your website, Instagram and email look like three different businesses.
- You've raised your prices or shifted who you work with, but your look still says "just getting started."
- You feel a little reluctant to share *anything* branded, not only your website.
- Every new graphic or post feels like starting from scratch, because there are no settled rules for how things should look.

If this sounds familiar, a new website alone won't fix it. A site built on a brand that no longer fits simply carries the same problem into a nicer layout.

### Signs the website is the problem

- You still like your colors, fonts and logo. The brand feels like you.
- But the site is awkward on a phone, has outdated information, or makes visitors work to figure out what you offer.
- The way to book a call is buried, or there are several competing buttons and no obvious next step.
- People compliment your Instagram, but the website doesn't bring in inquiries.
- You've outgrown the template it was built on, or you can't easily update it yourself.

Here, a website refresh is usually enough. Keep the brand, and rebuild the structure and the words so the site does its job. If this is where you are, [Why Your Website Isn't Converting](/post/why-your-website-isn-t-converting) goes into the most common culprits.

### When it's both

This is common when a business has grown a lot since its website first went up. Your services have matured, your clients have changed, and neither the brand nor the site has caught up. [When Your Website No Longer Matches the Level You're Operating At](/post/when-your-website-no-longer-matches-the-level-you-re-operating-at) covers what that gap tends to look like.

If it's both, the order matters: brand first, then website. So many design decisions on a website depend on your colors and fonts that building the site first usually means redoing parts of it later. Doing the two together tends to be quicker and less expensive than doing them separately, for exactly that reason.

### A five-minute test

Put three things side by side: your homepage, your most recent Instagram post and your email signature. Then ask yourself two questions.

1. **Do these look like they come from the same business?**
2. **If my ideal client saw all three, would she feel this is for someone at her level?**

If the answer to the first question is no, start with the brand. If the answer is yes but your website still isn't bringing in bookings, the website is where to focus.
