One of the most common reasons I hear for putting off a new website is "I need to get professional photos done first."

I understand why. You want to look the part. But a photo shoot takes time, money and a fair bit of courage, so it slides down the list, and your website stays exactly as it is.

The good news is that you can have a polished website that feels like you without a professional shoot. Here's what I suggest to clients in that spot.

### Real photos of you still matter most

First, one thing worth saying. For a coach or service provider, people are deciding whether they'd like to work with *you*. A real photo of your face helps them feel that connection faster than anything else on the page.

So the goal is to find a way to show up as yourself, even if the photo didn't come from a studio.

### Your phone can take a great photo

Most phones today take photos that look lovely on a website. What usually makes a photo look homemade is the setup around it, and that's easy to fix.

A few things that make a big difference:

- **Face a window.** Soft daylight from the front or side is the most flattering light there is. Avoid overhead lights and direct midday sun.
- **Use the back camera.** It's sharper than the selfie camera. Set your phone on a stack of books or a cheap tripod and use the timer.
- **Hold it at eye level or slightly above.** Shooting from below is rarely kind to anyone.
- **Keep the background simple.** A plain wall, a bookshelf or a tidy corner of your office works well. Clear away clutter you wouldn't want a client to see.
- **Wear something in your brand colors.** Solid colors photograph better than busy patterns, and they tie the photo to the rest of your site.
- **Take a lot of shots.** Move a little, laugh, look away, look back. The natural ones in between are often the keepers.

If you can, ask a friend to help. Most people relax more when someone else is behind the camera.

### Show your work, your space and your hands

Not every photo on your site needs to be a headshot. Some of the best images for a service business show what working with you feels like:

- Your desk with your notebook, laptop and a cup of coffee
- Your hands writing, sketching or holding a client workbook
- The space where you meet clients, or the view from your home office
- Materials, products or tools you use in your work

They're easy to take on your phone and give your pages variety without you being in every shot.

### Simple brand graphics can fill the gaps

Some sections of a website don't need a photo at all. A quote from a client, a short list of what's included in a package or a single bold sentence about who you help can sit on a block of your brand color and look intentional.

When those graphics use the same colors and fonts as the rest of your site, everything feels connected, even with only a few photos.

### A careful word about AI images

AI tools can now take a photo you already have and turn it into a more polished version, with better lighting or a cleaner background. Used lightly, that can help. Starting from a real photo of yourself keeps it honest.

What I'd steer away from is an AI image of a person who isn't you, presented as you. Visitors are getting good at spotting them. AI works best for supporting images, like a textured background or an illustration for a blog post.

### Stock photos, used sparingly

Stock photos can work for backgrounds or to illustrate an idea. Just avoid the obvious ones (the handshake, the woman laughing at a salad) and never use stock in place of your own face on your About or Home page.

### Fewer, better images

You don't need dozens of photos. A small website with a clear home page, services page and booking page might only need four or five good images in total.
