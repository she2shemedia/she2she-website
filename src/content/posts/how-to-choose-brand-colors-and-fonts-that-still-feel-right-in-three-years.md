Think back to a logo or website you loved a few years ago. Chances are, it now looks a little dated. The trendy gradient, the skinny script font, the color that was everywhere for one season: they all have a shelf life.

When you're a small business owner, you don't have the budget to redo your branding every eighteen months. You need colors and fonts that still feel like you three years from now. The good news is that choosing well is less about talent and more about a few simple decisions.

### Start With Your Clients, Not Your Favorites

It's tempting to pick the colors you personally love. But your brand isn't a bedroom makeover. It's a signal to the people you want to work with.

Ask yourself:

- How do I want my clients to feel when they land on my website?
- Do I want to come across as calm, bold, polished, warm or playful?
- What do my ideal clients already respond to?

A grounded business coach and a vibrant social media trainer probably shouldn't look identical, even if they both love teal.

### Build a Small, Flexible Palette

You don't need ten colors. Most strong brands work with four or five:

- **One main color.** The one people remember you by.
- **One or two supporting colors.** These add depth without competing.
- **A soft neutral.** Cream, warm gray or soft white gives your design room to breathe.
- **A dark text color.** Pure black works, but a deep charcoal or plum can feel softer.

Before you commit, test your colors where they'll actually live: on a phone screen, on a white background, next to your photo. Make sure text is easy to read. A beautiful color that's hard to read on a button isn't doing its job.

### Be Careful With Trends

Trends aren't bad. They just age quickly. A good rule of thumb: let your main color and fonts be timeless, and let trends show up in the small, easy-to-change places, such as a seasonal graphic, a social media template or a background pattern.

If a choice feels exciting mostly because you've seen it everywhere this month, give it a week. If you still love it, it may have staying power.

### Choose Fonts That Do Their Job

Fonts carry personality, but their first job is to be readable. Here's a simple approach:

- **Pick two fonts, three at most.** One for headlines and one for body text is plenty.
- **Make them clearly different.** A classic serif headline with a clean sans-serif body is a pairing that rarely goes wrong.
- **Skip fonts that are hard to read at small sizes.** Highly decorative scripts are lovely for a word or two, but they can wear readers out in a full paragraph.
- **Choose fonts you can use everywhere.** Your website, Instagram graphics, email and printed pieces should all be able to use them.

### Do the Three-Year Test

Once you have a draft, try this:

- **Would I be proud of this on a stranger's screen?** If a potential client saw it with no explanation, would it feel professional?
- **Does it still work if my business grows?** Could it stretch from coaching to courses, or from one service to five?
- **Does it feel like me, or like a template?** The best brands have a little personality that can't be copied.

If you can say yes to all three, you're likely on solid ground.

### Consistency Matters More Than Perfection

Here's something I tell the women I work with: a good palette used consistently beats a perfect palette used randomly. Once you've chosen your colors and fonts, use them everywhere, the same way each time. That repetition is what makes people recognize you.
